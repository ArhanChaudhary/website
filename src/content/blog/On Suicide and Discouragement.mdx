These are some thoughts I wrote to my friends late at night that I have always felt strongly towards. I've left this article private because I do not want my close friends and family to read it. Please do not read this article only if you are uncomfortable with topics pertaining to suicide.

"I have recently been thinking a lot about the concept of suicide, and how it being a taboo subject imposes what I interpret to be a double standard that has made me feel angry and alienated. Maybe going down this line of thinking till traumatize me in the future but at least writing this out makes me feel cathartic so hey.

The narrative goes like this: you shouldn't commit suicide because it will destroy your friends and family; committing suicide is the most selfish thing you can do so you shouldn't do it. This was taught to me during health class in high school, during a time period when I was briefly suicidal, and it genuinely made me feel so angry. Feeling suicidal is a strictly a product of your personal life and your own feelings; now you're justifying why I shoudn't feel this way by guilt-tripping me and asking me to spread emotional bandwidth to care about my family. Irrespective of how much I actually care about my family, these are responsibilities that I *did not ask for*. This is not good advice that makes me feel better; it feels shallow, patronizing, and manipulative. It is reminiscent of abusive relationship. I have always thought that it was unfair that I have to care about how other people feel over how I am allowed to feel or what I am allowed to do. I've felt alone in that there is nothing I can do about it: obviously my family wouldn't try to justify any of these ideas if I were to tell them I was suicidal, and my family is omnipresent in my life, so there is no way to relinquish any of the responsibility. Once you begin to feel this way there is no way out.

I disagree with the notion that suicide is never the answer. I think that nine times out of ten, pain is temporary, and there is generally much to look forward to in life, but there are scenarios where I don't think that is true. If you are sentenced to prison for life under false pretenses, or you are forever condemned to grueling labor by an authoritarian regime, or you have crippiling paralysis, I think it is okay to tell yourself that you are not strong enough to frontload misery and suffering for the chance of having a better life at a distant point in the future. I'm not necessarily saying that this is the always the best choice, or that people should give up, but that it should be an option which we can respect in a better way than "suicide is never the answer" as a society. We literally euthanize chronically ill pets and make the decision to end their life for them but we are suddenly incapable of ever making that decision for ourselves: there are some countries where you could be stuck with crippling pain that will chain you to a hospital bed forever.

There was a recent case of a YouTuber who, after being diagnosed with fibromyalgia (a condition charaterized by chronic pain) for more than four years, and after medical treatment became "financially impossible", told his audience that he wanted to commit suicide. And do you want to guess how his audience reacted? With parasocial repulsion and manipulation, and while not everyone was trying to be intentionally malicious there was a crowd of his friends/audience that gave different flavors of the above narrative. He later says that he was "not thinking clearly" and wants to reassess his choice. He says his audience's reaction had made him feel ashamed that he felt that way to begin with. He finally says that he does not want to emotionally hurt his audience.

Is this not *fucking* crazy to anyone else? How do you think he ACTUALLY feels right now. So he decides to be open about the most personal, most psychologically painful topic of suicide to his audience, which has probably been on his mind for years, and is rewarded by imaginary people saying they cared about you so much that they would feel sad or distressed if he were to commit suicide. His retraction was not motivated by any amount of actually feeling better or mentally well; he was trapped in an cage and forced to make that statement, going so far as to apologize for daring felt that way. How do you think he feels about ever being open again?

Now to clarify, I am not currently suicidal. My mental health has truthfully not been doing well, but I am always cogniziant of the fact that I live a life of privelege and opportunity. I see that there is more in the world worth living for. I've just had these thoughts about suicide for a long time."
