You write:> Secondly, I may be wrong, but I don’t think LLMs can come up with new ideas the way we do. In the example we saw in this post, the ideas were already there, they just needed some refinement
A few others share this opinion. My favorite interpretation is from Nestor Guillen's article. He refers to LLMs' capabilities as encompassing the "convex hull of ideas." You can read his article for his elaboration, and I am going to assume that is what you meant. This was my belief until Anthropic solved the GRH using Claude; now, I am skeptical of this belief for a few reasons.
First, what makes an idea "new"? I think this distinction is important, because it can be easy to subconsciously bias the definition of "new" to fit a belief. Ideas build upon other ideas, so it's not very clear to me how you define "new ideas the way we do". This excerpt is from Alpoge's remark:I had us [I and Claude] on the line of combining two quite different ideas present in a toy setting, one of which was already new and shocking, making very simple one of the shadows of Riemann over which we mathematicians felt most triumph. ... In Stepanov’s argument resolving Riemann over function fields one uses a divisor involving multiplicity. This argument is a descendant of Stepanov’s, but multiplicity is hard to find over the integers, corresponding to something like differentiating whole numbers.
